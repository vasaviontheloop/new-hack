How to help

  1. Aphasia affects language, not intelligence. The person knows exactly what they mean.
  2. Give time. Let them choose the clues. Don't finish the word unless they ask.
  3. Wrong clues are normal. Saying a related word, like "fork" for "spoon", is common. WordReach expects it.
  4. Use "Help me say it". It gives the first sound, then the parts, then the whole word.
  5. Use the word right away. Tap a sentence so the word becomes part of the conversation.

WordReach supports communication. It is not a medical device and doesn't replace a speech-language therapist. Sudden difficulty finding words can be a sign of stroke. Call emergency services.
